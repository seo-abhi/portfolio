CoinRemitter SEO Case Study

Strengthening SEO for an Established Crypto Payment Platform

When I started working on CoinRemitter in March 2024, I was entering a very different SEO situation from most of the newer websites in my portfolio.

CoinRemitter was already an established cryptocurrency payment platform with an existing SEO strategy that was performing well.

There was no reason to rebuild everything simply for the sake of creating a new strategy.

Instead, as part of the SEO team, my responsibility was to identify the parts of the existing search ecosystem where focused improvements could create additional value.

My work concentrated around high-intent commercial pages, on-page SEO, page speed and Core Web Vitals, content, internal linking, and off-page SEO.

I worked on the project from March 2024 to November 2025 as an SEO Executive.

The Starting Point

CoinRemitter was not an SEO project starting from zero. The website already had:

  • Existing search visibility
  • An established product
  • Existing SEO activity
  • Commercial pages
  • Informational content
  • A functioning SEO strategy

That completely changed how I approached the project. The objective was not: Replace What Already Works It was: Identify the Gaps → Strengthen Them → Support the Existing SEO Ecosystem The main areas where I saw opportunities included:

  • Page-speed and Core Web Vitals problems
  • Weak or inconsistent metadata on selected pages
  • Commercial pages that needed stronger keyword-focused content
  • Weak keyword placement on some pages
  • Limited content coverage around valuable search opportunities
  • Weak internal connections between blogs and commercial pages
  • A backlink profile with room to grow

This became a project about improving specific parts of an established SEO system rather than rebuilding the entire system.

The Initial SEO Problems

Unlike a new marketplace or fresh ecommerce store, CoinRemitter did not have SEO problems across every part of the website. The opportunities were more focused.

On-Page SEO

Selected pages needed improvement around:

  • Meta titles
  • Meta descriptions
  • Page content
  • Keyword targeting
  • Keyword placement
  • Heading hierarchy
  • FAQs
  • Internal links
  • Anchor text
  • Image alt text
  • Canonicals
  • Content freshness

The most recurring issues I personally worked on were: Metadata + Page Content + Keyword Placement

Technical SEO

The biggest technical area I personally focused on was:

Page Speed & Core Web Vitals

Performance issues were connected to:

  • Heavy images
  • Fonts
  • Visual assets
  • Large CSS files
  • Large JavaScript files
  • Third-party scripts
  • Some server-side factors

This required regular coordination with the development team.

Content

CoinRemitter already had content, but there were opportunities to create more search-focused articles around:

  • Crypto payment problems
  • Widget integrations
  • Business use cases
  • Long-tail questions
  • Commercial features

There was also room to connect informational content more deliberately with CoinRemitter's product ecosystem.

Internal Linking

Useful blog content existed, but stronger contextual relationships could be built between: Blogs → Gateways → Plugins → APIs → Widgets → Homepage This would help both users and search engines move more naturally through the website.

Off-Page SEO

The backlink profile also had room to grow relative to the competitiveness of the cryptocurrency payment market. My off-page focus therefore included:

  • Guest posts
  • PR
  • Website selection
  • Target-page planning
  • Anchor-text decisions
  • Placement coordination

My Role

I worked as part of the SEO team rather than acting as the sole owner of CoinRemitter's complete SEO strategy. My direct responsibilities included:

  • Keyword research

My contribution was to strengthen selected areas of that strategy, not claim ownership of work that was already being successfully managed by the wider SEO team.

Understanding Feature-Level Search Intent

A broad keyword such as:

“crypto payment gateway”

is extremely relevant to CoinRemitter. But it is also broad and competitive. The widget ecosystem created more specific opportunities around actual product functionality. Examples included:

  • pricing widget
  • presale widget
  • payment button widget
  • payment page widget
  • invoice services

These searches could be matched much more directly with dedicated product pages. For example, the current Pricing Widget page is specifically built around accepting cryptocurrency through tiered pricing plans, while the Presale Widget targets crypto payments for ICO and token-presale use cases.

Strengthening Metadata

  • Search intent

I optimized titles and descriptions around the relationship: Search Query → Search Snippet → Landing-Page Experience The goal was not to maximize keyword density. It was to make the snippet accurately communicate what the user would find after clicking.

A feature page needs to explain more than the product name. The Payment Page similarly explains its page-based payment experience, fixed and custom payments, branding, goal tracking, pricing options, integrations and use cases. Making Page Speed a Major SEO Priority

The largest technical SEO issue I personally focused on was website performance. CoinRemitter had page-speed and Core Web Vitals problems caused by a combination of:

  • Heavy images
  • Fonts
  • Design assets
  • Large CSS files
  • Large JavaScript files
  • Third-party scripts
  • Server-side performance factors

I audited the issues from the SEO and performance side and coordinated recommendations with developers. This became one of the most technically important parts of my contribution.

Improving Images and Visual Assets

Large images and visual resources were adding unnecessary page weight. I recommended improvements around:

  • Image compression
  • Reducing unnecessary image size
  • Optimizing visual assets
  • Reviewing fonts
  • Reducing avoidable front-end weight

The objective was straightforward: Less Data to Load → Faster User Experience particularly on important commercial pages.

Improving CSS and JavaScript

Large front-end files were another recurring performance factor. I coordinated with developers around:

  • CSS minification
  • JavaScript minification
  • Reducing unnecessary file weight
  • Reviewing scripts affecting loading performance

My role was to identify the performance issue, define the SEO and UX concern, and coordinate the appropriate development work.

Handling Third-Party and Server-Side Performance Factors

Some performance issues were deeper than normal on-page optimization. Where third-party scripts or server-side factors were involved, I:

  • Identified the issue
  • Documented the performance concern
  • Coordinated with developers
  • Reviewed the performance after implementation

The deeper engineering work was handled by the development team. I do not claim server-level implementation that was outside my SEO role.

Performance Outcome

After the combined optimization work, I observed an improvement in website performance compared with the earlier state. This supported:

  • Faster page experiences
  • Better usability
  • Stronger Core Web Vitals performance
  • A cleaner technical foundation

I no longer have the historical first-party performance dataset required to attach exact percentage improvements. For that reason, I do not claim: X% Speed Increase or: X% Ranking Increase Caused by Performance The result is presented at the level I can genuinely support: Website performance and Core Web Vitals improved after the coordinated optimization work.

The Most Difficult Part

The hardest part of CoinRemitter was not fixing one catastrophic SEO issue. It was knowing:

What Not to Change

The website already had a functioning SEO strategy. That meant aggressively restructuring pages, replacing every keyword, or rebuilding the entire content strategy could have disrupted things that were already working. The better approach was to identify specific weaknesses: Page Speed Widget Pages Content Coverage Internal Linking Backlinks and improve those areas without creating unnecessary instability elsewhere. That required a different kind of SEO judgement from working on a brand-new site. The question was not: How much can I change? It was: Where can I create additional value without disrupting what already works?

What I Learned

CoinRemitter reinforced an important SEO lesson:

An established SEO strategy does not always need to be replaced.

Sometimes the stronger decision is to understand what is already working and concentrate resources on the gaps. For CoinRemitter, those gaps included: High-Intent Page SEO Page Speed & Core Web Vitals Content Internal Linking Backlink Profile It also reinforced the value of feature-level search targeting. A broad term such as: crypto payment gateway may represent the overall business. But pages built around:

  • pricing widgets
  • presale payments
  • payment buttons
  • payment pages
  • invoices

can capture users with much more specific needs. The current product architecture still reflects this feature-level approach.

My Biggest Takeaway

From March 2024 to November 2025, I contributed to the SEO of CoinRemitter as part of the SEO team. The website already had an established search strategy. My responsibility was not to replace it. My job was to strengthen specific areas where I could create additional value. That included: Widget & Service-Page SEO Keyword Research On-Page Optimization Page Speed Core Web Vitals Blog Strategy Internal Linking Guest Posts PR GSC & GA4 Monitoring I no longer have access to the project's original first-party analytics, so I intentionally do not attach unverifiable historical traffic-growth percentages to this case study. The strongest representation of my contribution is the type of work I performed inside an already functioning SEO ecosystem: Improving technical performance, strengthening high-intent commercial pages, building better relationships between informational and product content, and supporting the website with a stronger backlink profile without unnecessarily rebuilding a strategy that was already working. For me, this project demonstrates that I can: Join an established SEO ecosystem, understand what already works, identify specific weaknesses, coordinate focused improvements across content, technical performance, and off-page SEO, and add value without creating unnecessary change.
